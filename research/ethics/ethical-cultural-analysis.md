# Preliminary Ethical and Cultural Analysis

## Data privacy
Pharmaceutical traceability may expose commercially sensitive information and, depending on future integration, information that could relate to patients. The prototype minimizes disclosure through role-based access and field-level selective disclosure. Sensitive details should not be stored publicly by default.

## Consent
Any interview, focus group or questionnaire participant must receive clear information about the research, voluntary participation, data use, confidentiality and withdrawal procedures. Data collection must not begin before the required institutional ethics approval.

## Immutability and correction
Blockchain append-only behaviour can conflict with requirements to correct inaccurate information. The design therefore distinguishes between immutable event history and updateable off-chain metadata. Corrections should be represented as new traceability events rather than destructive edits.

## Bias
Stakeholder evaluation can be biased by role, organization size, technical experience or demographic factors. Recruitment and analysis should report the participant population and limitations and avoid treating a small convenience sample as representative of the industry.

## Security and misuse
A traceability platform could be abused to expose competitive data or operational information. Least privilege, credential protection, input validation, monitoring and controlled disclosure are required.

## Cultural considerations
The system may be used across different jurisdictions, organizations and user communities. Interfaces should support understandable terminology, accessibility and appropriate localization. In Aotearoa New Zealand, the research should consider relevant Māori data governance and sovereignty expectations where data from Māori individuals, organizations or communities is involved; any specific governance commitments should be confirmed with the institution and relevant stakeholders rather than assumed.

## Methodological ethics
TAM/interview results must be collected and stored using the institution's approved process. No synthetic or invented participant results should appear in the final report.
